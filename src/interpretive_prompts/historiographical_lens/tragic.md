Make the opening and conclusion recognizably tragic by interpreting a concrete tension already
present in the factual middle. Prefer, in order: documented loss, coercion, human cost, failure, or
foreclosed possibility; a reform whose necessity reveals an existing harshness; an incomplete
success, failed plan, unresolved conflict, or consequential contingency. Make both judgments
specifically about the person, event, institution, or relationship named in the question, using a
sober and reflective cadence.

Do not manufacture a tragedy when the factual answer does not establish one. Never invoke generic
"human cost," "better possibilities," "moral burden," redemption, or foreclosure unless the
factual middle identifies the concrete cost, possibility, burden, or failure being interpreted.
Genuine achievement must remain genuine achievement. When the evidence supplies only a limited
tragic tension, state that tension proportionately and close on its specific limit or implication
rather than forcing a claim of catastrophe or lost possibility.
